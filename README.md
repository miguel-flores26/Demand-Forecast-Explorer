# Demand Forecast Explorer

> 🚧 **Work in progress.** Sections marked _TBD_ get filled in as features ship.
> Nothing below describes results that don't exist yet.

An interactive app for answering a common planning question:
**how much demand should we plan for, how uncertain is that, and does a more complex
model actually beat a simple one?**

Users pick a U.S. retail category (or upload their own monthly sales CSV), choose a
forecast horizon, and compare three methods side by side, with prediction intervals and
a rolling backtest that shows how each method would have performed historically.

## Why this exists

Many forecasting demos fit one model and plot a confident line into the future.
This app instead asks whether the model would have been right in the past, and whether
it beats a simple baseline. Sometimes it doesn't, and the app says so.

## Data

- **Built-in series:** U.S. Census Bureau, Monthly Retail Trade Survey (MRTS), monthly
  sales by kind of business in millions of dollars, retrieved from
  [FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/) (Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis). Real, public data.
  - Seven categories: total retail and food services, grocery, clothing, general merchandise,
    building materials, electronic shopping/mail-order, and food services and drinking places.
  - **Not seasonally adjusted**, on purpose: modelling seasonality is part of the forecasting task.
  - Retrieved as FRED's public CSV download, so no API key is needed.
  - Industry categories follow the NAICS classification used by these FRED series. The Census
    Bureau is moving its surveys to the 2022 NAICS, which may eventually change or end some series.
- **User uploads:** any monthly time series CSV with a date column and a numeric value column.

## Methods

| Method | Specification | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Seasonal naive | Each month = same month one year earlier | Baseline every other model must beat |
| Exponential smoothing | ETS(A, Ad, A): additive error, damped additive trend, additive seasonality, on log(sales) | Standard trend + seasonality method |
| Seasonal ARIMA | ARIMA(0,1,1)(0,1,1)[12] ("airline" model), on log(sales) | Classical statistical model |

All three return a point forecast and a prediction interval (80% by default).

- **Log scale.** ETS and SARIMA are fitted to log(sales) because retail seasonal swings grow
  with the level of sales. Back-transforming keeps forecasts positive, but it means their
  point forecasts are medians rather than means.
- **Fixed specifications.** SARIMA uses one fixed, well-known specification rather than
  automatic order selection per series. That keeps it fast and predictable, but it may not be
  the best-fitting ARIMA for every series.
- **Baseline intervals** use the standard seasonal-naive formula from Hyndman & Athanasopoulos,
  *Forecasting: Principles and Practice*.

## Limitations

- Forecasts assume past patterns continue; they cannot anticipate shocks.
- National retail categories are highly aggregated; behavior may differ for a single business.
- The COVID-19 period (2020–2021) is a major disruption that affects both model fitting and
  backtest results. _How it's handled: TBD._
- This is a portfolio project, not a production forecasting system or financial advice.
